Quick Ratio
0.90×
(Cash + AR) ÷ Current Liabilities
Dipped below 1.0 — watch AP timing
▼ was 0.94× in June
A stricter version of the Current Ratio that only counts cash and money customers owe us — not inventory, since that can take time to sell. At 0.90, there's 90 cents of quick cash for every $1 owed soon. Below 1.0 means the company would need to collect on receivables or dip into credit to cover bills without selling inventory.
Debt-to-Equity
0.39

Manageable; trending up
▲ was 0.37 in June
Compares what the company owes (debt) to what it actually owns (equity). At 0.39, there's 39 cents of debt for every $1 of owner equity — meaning the business is funded mostly by its own value, not borrowing. Lower is generally safer; this is a healthy, manageable level.

AR Up 12% — Reverses June's Collections Gain

AR rose from $114,724 to $128,250. Worth confirming invoice timing and following up on any aging balances.

Wailoa Inventory Dipped Negative

Wailoa's finished goods balance slipped to ($55) from $414. A small accounting correction is likely needed, similar to June's Kaiao/Production fix.

Hilo & Smith St. Led the Sell-Through

Hilo Store (−$58K), Hilo Web (−$24K), and Sig On Smith (−$54K) posted the largest inventory drawdowns — a good sign for July sales at the flagship locations.

LOC fell from $144,060 to $141,179. Net liquid cushion (Cash − LOC) is $127,118, down from $150,136 as cash also declined.

KU'ILUA LLC Investment Jumped

Employee Advances Slightly Up

Strong Asset Base Remains Intact
